# From findings to a review report

Use after appraisal and adjudication. The form and audience follow V1/V3;
presentation is S3. Evidence, fairness, proportionality, and handoff follow
H2/H5/H6/H8. Use the local venue-specific report template in the forms collection.

## Open with an accurate overview

An opening summary is an S3 default, not a universal required paragraph. It may
help organize the reviewer's reasoning and can be shortened or omitted when the
actual form or task calls for a different structure. Supported strengths and
decision-relevant concerns still need to be communicated where the form requires.

Briefly describe the question, approach, principal findings, and contribution
as understood from the reviewed material. Add supported strengths and a concise
assessment of the main limitations. This lets authors recognize what was reviewed
and locate the reasoning behind later comments. Do not invent praise or a
literature-verified novelty judgment. Use contribution/importance criteria only
when appropriate to the article type and venue.

The overview must agree with the detailed comments. If methods are insufficiently
reported, do not simultaneously claim that their execution was verified. Do not
copy a training example's verdict or announce an editorial decision.

The review-article slide suggests an opening with strengths, weaknesses and a
succinct recommendation. Apply that recommendation placement only in a field
whose readership permits it; author-facing prose need not reveal confidential
editor advice. A courteous opening must not invent strengths, dilute an established
problem or add a negative concern merely for balance.

## Organize substantive concerns

Prefer the most consequential issues first, or group by manuscript section when
that makes the report easier to answer. Both are S3 options. Keep stable IDs and
priority visible; the venue's form takes precedence over a major/minor layout.
Conference weaknesses and decision-relevant questions need not become journal
revision categories.

Each substantive comment connects:

1. An inspected location and precise observation.
2. Evidence or an applicable methodological criterion.
3. Consequence for a claim or ability to evaluate it.
4. The smallest adequate response, with acceptable alternatives where possible.

Replace “I assume validation is independent” with a bounded request to identify
the split and explain its suitability. A concern may be a reporting gap without
proving an analytical error. Request a new experiment only when necessary for
retained claims or applicable criteria; consider clarification, reanalysis, or
claim narrowing first. Label research extensions optional (H6).

## Separate minor corrections by consequence

Use concise, located requests for ambiguous wording, reference errors, figure
legibility, or technical clarifications with limited effects on interpretation.
Avoid exhaustive copyediting unless separately requested as author-side work.
Reassess priority if an apparently local issue changes central evidence: a missing
uncertainty estimate or disagreement between text and figure is not automatically
minor. Do not generate minor comments to meet an imagined quota.

## Align channels, ratings, and recommendation

Keep ordinary scientific objections answerable by authors, including reasons
underlying a negative recommendation. Send conflicts and sensitive process or
integrity observations through the actual restricted channel. Default journal
recommendation placement is editor-facing unless the form specifies otherwise;
conference rating visibility depends on stage and field. Private does not mean
permanently secret if venue policy permits later publication.

Use only actual recommendation categories or scale anchors. Explain the relation
between evidence, remediability, venue criteria, and advice. Do not count comments
or turn reviewer confidence into acceptance probability. A recommended major
revision is advice, not a promise of acceptance.

The slide's “minor if no re-review is needed” is a useful journal heuristic, not
a definition or permission to skip a requested re-review. Judge the scientific
effect and remediability, apply the journal's categories, and explain whether
specialist rechecking is needed. Even a localized correction can require
verification. Conference scores and response stages do not inherit journal
minor/major revision labels.

Scientific need for re-review and the reviewer's availability are separate.
If the work needs another scientific check but the human cannot take it on,
retain the evidence-based advice and state that staffing limit for the editor.
Do not downgrade the concern, choose acceptance or promise a future review merely
to accommodate availability. The editor decides how to arrange further review.

Required versus optional requests are separate from minor versus major priority:
a minor correction can be required, and an interesting extension can be optional.
Use stable numbered IDs; provide page/line/panel locations when available without
inventing them. Do not hide the required scientific remedy only in confidential
editor notes. The actual policy determines who sees those notes and whether they
may later be released; do not promise that authors will never see them.

For tone and faithful wording, use [professional language rules](professional-language.md).
For whether manuscript language blocks assessment, use
[language and readability](../02-appraisal/language-and-readability.md).

## Deliver a usable draft

Remove template instructions, duplicate criticisms, unsupported assertions, and
unnecessary empty headings. State actual coverage and checks not performed.
Keep private notes separate from author-facing output. Label the draft for human
verification, apply applicable disclosure and retention rules, and leave final
judgment and submission to the accountable human.
