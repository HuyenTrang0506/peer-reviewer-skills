# Integrity concerns and overlapping work

Apply H2/H5 in [the rulebook](rulebook.md). This chapter clarifies the limits of
the supplied training slide's “identified plagiarism” and reject advice.

| ID | Class / trigger | Rule and action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| INT-H1 Describe evidence | H: suspected plagiarism, duplication or other integrity issue | Record the exact inspected overlap/anomaly, public source, attribution context and uncertainty. Similar topics, a percentage, a low novelty judgment or a visual resemblance alone do not establish misconduct. Do not claim plagiarism detection or forensic checks not performed. |
| INT-H2 Respect process | H: escalating a concern | Use the designated restricted editor/AC/PC/ethics route. Do not accuse authors, investigate identities, contact third parties, upload confidential text to detection services, or demand an automatic rejection. Any external action needs authorization and venue permission. |
| INT-V1 Applicable policy | V: venue defines preprints, duplicate publication, text reuse, contemporaneous work or prior-review transfer | Apply that policy to the evidence and distinguish it from a scientific contribution judgment. A preprint or cited standard-method passage is not automatically improper overlap. |
| INT-S1 Useful differentiation | S: prior work is relevant but no misconduct is established | Explain the specific contribution or attribution question and a bounded clarification request. Do not manufacture novelty by requesting cosmetic differences or claim reframing unsupported by evidence. |

Editors/committees determine investigation and decisions. If an actual venue
criterion is demonstrably unmet, reasoned reviewer advice can say so; it must not
replace that process with a universal similarity threshold. Importance and breadth
also depend on article type and venue. A local case study or narrow application
can contribute valid contextual evidence without supporting broad generalization;
do not treat the slide's “parochial research” label as a universal rejection rule.
