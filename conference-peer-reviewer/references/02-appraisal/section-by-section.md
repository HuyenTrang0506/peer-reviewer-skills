# Section-by-section appraisal

Use this chapter during evidence appraisal, not as a compulsory list of faults.
Select questions by article type, claim, and applicable venue criteria (V1/V2).
The section order is a navigation aid (S1); evidence and coverage honesty remain
required (H2/H3). Non-IMRaD work can map these functions to its own structure.

## Article type before checklist

Read the venue's definition of this article type before assessing contribution.
A clinical educational overview may contribute through accurate synthesis,
coverage, and practical relevance; it need not report a novel experiment or
challenge a paradigm. Do not transfer a selective original-research novelty
threshold to an educational article, replication, protocol, or case report.
Conversely, do not assume every review article is exempt from originality
criteria: verify the actual instructions. Scientific validity, venue fit,
novelty, and reader importance are separate judgments.

Select reporting guidance by design, with the applicable version/extensions:
for example, CONSORT for randomized trials or PRISMA for systematic reviews.
Use the routing table in [reviewer-methodology.md](reviewer-methodology.md).
An educational/narrative overview is not automatically a systematic review.
Checklist omissions support questions about reporting; they do not alone prove
that procedures were omitted or that the study is invalid.

For literature-synthesis manuscripts, use [review-article rules](review-articles.md)
to distinguish narrative, systematic, scoping and quantitative synthesis. Do not
skip methods or statistics simply because a manuscript is called a review.

## Title and abstract

Does the title identify the actual scope/design without overstating causality?
Does the abstract faithfully summarize the question, methods, principal findings,
uncertainty, and justified conclusion? Check against the inspected main text,
not just against the abstract's own assertions. Protocols have planned outcomes,
not completed results; assess them accordingly.

Check whether the aim and pivotal methodological details are present, and whether
the abstract introduces a result or claim absent from the inspected main paper.
A concise abstract need not repeat every detail; identify discrepancies that
change the meaning rather than enforce an arbitrary content quota.

## Introduction and prior work

Is the question explicit and is its importance supported? Is the reasoning from
existing knowledge to the question coherent? Are the most relevant prior results
represented accurately, including evidence inconsistent with the framing?
Distinguish a checked literature omission from a literature search not performed.
Suggest specific references only when they materially support a point, without
coercive self-citation or demands for citation volume.

## Methods and ethics

Are design, sampling, independent unit, measurements, analysis, and procedural
detail sufficient to appraise and, where applicable, repeat the work? Are they
appropriate for the question, rather than merely described in detail?
Inspect relevant authorized protocols/supplements before asserting absence.

For human/animal work, check applicable approval/exemption, consent, welfare, and
privacy reporting. Requirements depend on study and jurisdiction; absent reporting
is not proof of an unethical study. Route sensitive process/integrity concerns
through the venue's designated channel without unsupported accusations (H5).

## Results, tables, and figures

Trace central results to the described analysis. Check units, denominators,
independent observations, missingness, uncertainty definitions, legends, axes,
and agreement between text and displays. Ask whether calibration and validation
are appropriately separated for the claimed generalization; separation by time
or site is not universally adequate or universally required.

Check the correspondence in both directions: results for methods/analyses
described, and methods for results reported. Distinguish a missing explanation
from a documented prespecified analysis not reported, and from an explicitly
labelled exploratory analysis. Do not infer selective reporting or invent
preregistration from an unexplained mismatch. For display-level checks use
[figure and table rules](figures-and-tables.md).

A missing interval or text/figure discrepancy can be major when it changes a
central inference, or minor when it is a localized presentation correction.
Do not inherit a severity label from a training example. State the exact
discrepancy and consequence instead of “something seems wrong.”

## Discussion, conclusion, and limitations

Do interpretations follow from the design and results? Are plausible alternative
explanations addressed, limitations acknowledged, and generalization bounded?
Is the contribution described against relevant evidence? Treat “does it matter?”
or “does it challenge a paradigm?” as prompts to interpret applicable criteria,
not universal acceptance tests. Candid limitations are not by themselves grounds
for rejection. Distinguish essential corrections from future research.

Check whether the research question is answered, what the findings add in context,
and whether conflicts with inspected prior findings are reasonably discussed.
Identify unaddressed limitations with evidence. A review article can contribute
through synthesis or new interpretation; broad generalization is not a universal
requirement. Do not dismiss a local application solely because its setting is
narrow. For comprehensibility and language limits use
[language and readability rules](language-and-readability.md).

## References and cross-section reconciliation

Check pivotal sources against the claims they support when access and scope
permit. Separate internal citation consistency from external verification.
Reconcile outcome definitions, sample counts, claims, and uncertainty across
abstract, methods, results, displays, discussion, and inspected supplements.
Log supported issues once, even when they appear in several sections.

## Chapter output

Record a claim/evidence map and supported issue candidates, each with location,
observation, consequence, and smallest adequate remedy. Record strengths and
limits too. Then adjudicate consequential concerns before drafting the report.
