# Experimental and clinical appraisal

Use the sections matching the actual claims. This guide supplies questions, not proof that a common failure occurred. Consult applicable reporting guidance and specialist expertise for the actual design.

## Independent units and causal inference

Trace allocation, treatment, measurement, and analysis units separately. Wells within a donor, cells within an animal, repeated visits, and patients within randomized clinics can be dependent. Ask how the analysis handles that dependence; do not presume every hierarchical design requires the same model. Check the number of independent units, paired structure, controls, exclusions, and timing.

For randomized interventions, distinguish sequence generation, allocation concealment, and masking. Missing reporting of one is not evidence that none occurred. For observational causal claims, inspect the target comparison, time zero, confounder choice/measurement, reverse causation, and selection. Do not insist that every observational paper become a randomized trial; narrowing to an association may be sufficient.

## Outcomes, estimates, and null claims

Align the stated primary outcome, timepoint, estimand, analysis population, and protocol where available. Inspect deviations, attrition, missing-data handling, multiplicity, and harms as applicable. Ask whether plausible assumptions could change the central inference rather than demand every conceivable sensitivity analysis.

A nonsignificant test does not show equivalence. Compare intervals with scientifically important effects; equivalence/non-inferiority needs a justified margin and suitable analysis. Inconclusive precision should lead to bounded interpretation, not retrospective observed-power calculations. A change in statistical significance alone does not prove two effects differ.

## Chemistry, assays, and mechanism — when central

Trace identity/purity/characterization to the entity responsible for the reported effect. Ask whether blank/negative/positive or orthogonal controls distinguish the proposed explanation from credible alternatives. A performance improvement alone may not establish a molecular mechanism.

For catalysis/electrochemistry, inspect normalization, loading, reference conditions, product balance, stability, and uncertainty where the claim depends on them. For biological probes, distinguish binding/target engagement, specificity, and downstream phenotype. For other assays, use their validated domain conventions rather than transfer these examples indiscriminately.

If spectra, images, acquisition parameters, or raw traces were not available, limit the assessment accordingly. Do not infer identity, purity, or manipulation from a caption. Request a chemist/assay specialist when a decisive interpretation exceeds competence.

## Keep the remedy tied to the claim

| Evidence encountered | Justified response | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Replicate type unspecified | Ask for biological/technical counts and analytical unit | Declare pseudoreplication as fact |
| Methods explicitly pool dependent replicates as independent | Request an appropriate analysis and reconciled uncertainty/claims | Assert that the direction of effect is necessarily false |
| Association described as proof of prevention | Explain identification limits; request narrower wording or justified analysis | Require a new trial regardless of the retained claim |
| Wide interval includes important benefit and harm | Request an inconclusive interpretation | Demand authors prove “no effect” from p > 0.05 |
| Mechanism asserted from one nonspecific assay | Identify the alternative explanation and minimum resolving evidence, or temper mechanism | Demand a standard battery unrelated to this mechanism |
