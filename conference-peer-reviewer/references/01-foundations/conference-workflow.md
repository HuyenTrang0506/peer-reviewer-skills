# Conference review workflow

## Conference review is venue- and track-specific

Bind the **edition/year, track, article type, and stage** before applying a rubric. Full-paper archival conferences, non-archival workshops, extended abstracts, clinical meeting abstracts, and journal-integrated tracks have different evidence budgets and publication paths. A fixed-cycle decision and rebuttal are common in some conferences, not universal. Do not characterize conference science as inherently less rigorous or presume journal-like revision rounds are available.

Inspect official reviewer instructions, track CFP, actual review form, supplementary-material rules, response/revision policy, anonymity, conflicts, AI-use rules, and deadlines. The checklist is a prompt for source collection, not proof a policy exists. If a material policy cannot be checked, name the gap and withhold the affected score/procedure instead of fabricating it.

## Map evidence to the actual form

1. Read the exact assigned version and allowed supplements. Identify the problem, claimed contribution, design, evidence, strengths, and limitations.
2. Assess soundness/quality, originality, significance, clarity, reproducibility, ethics, or other criteria only as defined by the form. Separate scientific validity from relevance and presentation. A theoretical paper needs justified proofs/assumptions rather than an arbitrary experimental battery; an abstract should be evaluated against that track's expected evidence, not an invented full-manuscript checklist.
3. Locate central concerns and make them answerable. Check the strongest defense before retaining a finding. Ask focused questions whose answers could change the assessment; label optional improvement suggestions separately.
4. Assign criterion scores and the overall recommendation using the supplied scale and anchors. Do not average criteria unless the venue instructs that. Confidence is a separate description of the reviewer's familiarity and verification depth, not a proxy for how strong the paper is.
5. Check consistency between ratings, written reasons, and outstanding decision-relevant questions. A positive recommendation can coexist with minor suggestions; a low score needs substantive reasons rather than dislike of the topic.

Reviewers advise; ACs/PCs or the venue's designated body decide. Do not forecast acceptance from a score, acceptance rate, or majority opinion. If the user requests a meta-review role, distinguish that authorized synthesis from an individual reviewer report.

## Rebuttal and discussion

Record the response window and whether clarifications, new experiments, revised PDFs, appendices, or external links are permitted. Some processes freeze the manuscript, while others allow revisions during discussion. Do not demand prohibited or infeasible work or silently treat an unrelated newer preprint as submitted evidence.

For each stable issue ID, compare the response with the permitted material: did it clarify a misunderstanding, supply an allowed analysis, narrow a claim, or leave a consequential concern? Verify actual changes where revisions are allowed; a promise to fix later is different from an available remedy. Record that distinction without assuming that every promised fix automatically invalidates the paper. Venue rules control whether an accepted conditional fix is possible.

Read peer reviews only when permitted and preserve independent initial reasoning where required. In discussion, identify factual/methodological disagreements and resolve them through evidence. Explain score changes and the reason an assessment stayed unchanged; do not require agreement or praise from authors. Escalate decisive expertise gaps to the appropriate chair without inventing expert consultation.

## Public and restricted channels

Record readership separately for the author review, numerical recommendation, confidence, ethics report, chair-only comment, discussion, and eventual public record. OpenReview is a platform, not a universal visibility policy. Confidentiality during review and later publication are different dimensions. Do not post a restricted concern into a public thread or hide ordinary scientific reasons from authors.

## Verified examples, not universal rules

- [NeurIPS 2025 reviewer guidelines](https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2025/ReviewerGuidelines), inspected 2026-10-22, define criterion ratings, an overall scale, a separate confidence scale, author response, reviewer/AC discussion, and public-release conditions. The instructions ask reviewers to identify questions whose answers could change their assessment and caution against penalizing candid limitations. Use the actual assigned edition's form rather than hardcode these 2025 scales.
- [ICLR 2026 reviewer guide](https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2026/ReviewerGuide), inspected 2026-10-22, permits discussion and revision, asks for a final updated recommendation, restricts requested experiments to validating existing content rather than significantly changing it, and says lack of SOTA alone is not grounds for rejection. Its contemporaneous-work and AI-disclosure policies are edition-specific; do not transfer its date cutoffs or tool permissions to another venue.
- ACM's peer-review policy endpoint returned HTTP 403 during this check. No claim of ACM/IEEE-wide policy verification is made. These two ML conference examples do not establish standards for all disciplines.

## Destination ambiguities

An OpenReview URL alone does not establish conference versus journal. A conference presentation based on a journal article may use a journal-first track with special rules. Ask for the track or provide a clearly bounded qualitative assessment until it is known. A reviewer does not decide a venue's artifact badge, oral/poster placement, or editorial acceptance unless that task and rubric were actually assigned.
