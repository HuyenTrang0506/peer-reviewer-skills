# Qualitative appraisal

Select this lens for qualitative claims, including a qualitative component of mixed-methods work. Name the methodological tradition when reported; do not assume that quantitative quality criteria transfer unchanged.

## Coherence and evidence

Check alignment between research question, epistemological position where relevant, sampling rationale, recruitment, collection context, analytic approach, and interpretation. Ask how the reported data support each central theme or account and how divergent or contradictory material was considered.

Distinguish an empirical observation, participant interpretation, researcher interpretation, and broad social/generalization claim. Quotations illustrate evidence but do not automatically establish the adequacy of sampling or analysis. Missing quotations can be a reporting issue; do not infer that interviews or analysis were fabricated.

## Reflexivity, sampling, and ethics

Assess researcher positioning, relationships, reflexivity, consent, privacy, and contextual details needed to interpret the findings. Consider deductive disclosure risks before requesting more participant detail or public transcripts.

Sample adequacy depends on the question and approach. Do not prescribe a universal participant minimum or require a saturation claim for every tradition. Likewise, intercoder agreement and member checking are not universal requirements; ask whether the chosen analytic approach is explained and coherent. For example, reflexive thematic analysis should not automatically be failed for lacking a kappa statistic.

## Proportionate requests

Request a clear account of recruitment and analysis if needed to appraise the stated conclusions. If a theme is supported only by selected examples and alternative accounts remain unexplained, ask for the analytic rationale and appropriately bounded interpretation. Do not require statistical representativeness from an explicitly contextual inquiry, or a new survey to validate every qualitative finding.

For mixed methods, assess how components were integrated and whether the combined inference follows from both; use the relevant quantitative lens only for that component. Verify applicable SRQR/COREQ guidance and venue requirements without using checklist compliance as a substitute for methodological judgment.
