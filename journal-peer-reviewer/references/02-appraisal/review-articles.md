# Appraising review articles

Use for manuscripts synthesizing existing literature, in either venue package.
This module appraises a review article; it does not conduct a new literature
review on the author's behalf. Apply the [rulebook](../01-foundations/rulebook.md)
and actual venue/article-type requirements. The REV rules below refine evidence,
fairness and proportionality obligations; they do not create a universal rubric.

## Select the synthesis type first

| Manuscript type | Focus of appraisal | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Narrative, educational or critical review | Scope, rationale for literature selection, accuracy, balanced coverage, synthesis, interpretation and usefulness for the intended audience | Automatically requiring a systematic search, flow diagram, pooled estimate or new experiments |
| Perspective, opinion or trends review | Declared viewpoint or purpose, accuracy of factual claims, appropriate attribution, recent developments where relevant, and contribution for the intended audience | Assuming every perspective needs no references, treating opinion as consensus, or requiring every historical paper in a focused trends review |
| Systematic review without meta-analysis | Question, eligibility, search coverage/dates, selection, extraction, appropriate bias appraisal and synthesis methods; whether conclusions follow from the included evidence | Skipping methods because there is no pooling, or requiring pooling when studies cannot meaningfully be combined |
| Meta-analysis | The review process above plus compatible populations/designs/outcomes, effect measures, dependence or overlapping cohorts, synthesis model, uncertainty, heterogeneity and justified sensitivity/bias analyses | Treating statistical significance or a precise pooled estimate as proof of high-quality evidence |
| Scoping or evidence-mapping review | Scope, eligibility, search/selection, charting and mapping consistent with its stated purpose | Automatically imposing intervention-effect conclusions or a mandatory risk-of-bias assessment unrelated to the purpose; investigate stronger claims when made |
| Other or hybrid synthesis | The stated question, actual selection/synthesis procedure, claims and relevant disciplinary conventions | Forcing an unfamiliar design into a narrative/systematic binary or accepting its label without checking what it does |

Meta-analysis is an analysis technique, not a synonym for systematic review;
systematic reviews need not include it. Narrative reviews can describe selection
methods and analyze published evidence. The slides' “no methods/data/statistics”
is therefore not a general exemption. If the type is unclear, ask a bounded
question about intended scope/procedure and limit type-specific judgments.

## Calibrate literature coverage to the article contract

Record the promised scope, synthesis type, search/literature cutoff where stated,
intended audience and actual venue expectations before judging references.

| Intended article | Appropriate coverage question | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Perspective or opinion | Are checkable factual claims and borrowed ideas supported or attributed as needed for this format? | Some venues permit reference-free pieces; that is not a blanket exemption from factual accuracy or attribution. Do not impose a citation quota or assume every opinion needs a full evidence synthesis. |
| Trends or focused update | Does it fairly cover developments important to the stated topic and period, with enough earlier context to explain them? | Comprehensive recent coverage within scope does not mean citing every paper ever published. Identify consequential omissions with evidence, not a reference-count threshold. |
| Systematic or explicitly exhaustive synthesis | Do search sources, eligibility, dates and selection support the claimed coverage? | Assess coverage within the declared question and methods; no search can justify an unchecked claim of capturing every publication. A word limit does not waive methods needed to support a completeness claim. |

Author perspective and neutral synthesis are different editorial purposes. A
permitted argumentative position is not itself evidence of bias; factual claims
and counterevidence still need fair treatment. Conversely, do not rewrite a
neutral evidence summary into the reviewer's preferred opinion. Apply REV-V3.

## Hard rules — evidence and interpretation

| ID | Trigger | Requirement and response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| REV-H1 Match claims to synthesis | A manuscript makes systematic, exhaustive, quantitative, causal or general claims | Appraise the selection and synthesis needed to support that claim, regardless of article label. Do not waive methodological assessment or assume a search is exhaustive. If procedure is unclear, identify the reporting gap before asserting flawed conduct. |
| REV-H2 Represent sources faithfully | Criticizing a summary of prior findings or using it to justify a major concern | Check pivotal accessible sources against the attributed population, design, results, uncertainty and limitations. Distinguish direct verification from abstract-only or second-hand reading. Do not invent source checks or call a study misrepresented without inspected evidence. |
| REV-H3 Separate epistemic status | Evaluating claims presented as fact or consensus | Distinguish supported findings, field consensus, disputed interpretation and author hypothesis. Do not establish consensus by counting citations or treating a prominent opinion as settled fact. Ask for appropriately bounded wording and supporting evidence where status is overstated. |
| REV-H4 Fairness with evidence | Alleging selective coverage, outdatedness or omission | Identify relevant omitted/contradictory evidence, its scope and consequence when verified. A missing familiar citation or a small reference count does not alone establish bias. If literature coverage was not independently checked, state that limit rather than declare completeness or cherry-picking. |
| REV-H5 Handle source status accurately | A pivotal cited study is disputed, corrected or retracted | Verify the actual notice/status where possible and evaluate how it affects the specific claim. An alleged or unverified status is not a fact. Historical discussion may legitimately cite a retracted study with context; do not demand indiscriminate deletion or imply guilt by citation. |
| REV-H6 Keep contribution and remedies proportionate | Assessing originality, overlap or required revisions | Assess synthesis, perspective, conceptual integration, updated evidence or audience value against the actual criteria. Do not require discovery of a new empirical result, an entirely new project or a preferred citation set merely because the work is a review. Constructive review can still support a justified negative recommendation. |

## Venue-conditional requirements

| ID | Trigger | Apply when established |
| --- | --- | --- |
| REV-V1 Review category and contribution | The venue defines review subtypes, intended readers, contribution threshold or format | Use that definition. Invitation or prior-consultation requirements are editorial eligibility matters where policy establishes them; an invitation does not validate the science. Do not assume reviews are universally invitation-only or universally accepted by conference tracks. |
| REV-V2 Applicable reporting guidance | Study design and venue make a checklist/extension relevant | Locate the applicable version, such as PRISMA for systematic reviews or PRISMA-ScR for scoping reviews, and verify its scope. PRISMA is reporting guidance, not a validity score or a mandatory template for every narrative review. Do not claim that a version or checklist was checked when it was not. |
| REV-V3 Editorial stance | Instructions specify an author perspective, critical argument, neutral summary or another editorial purpose | Assess the requested purpose without confusing author interpretation with established evidence. If expectations are unknown and materially affect critique, mark the uncertainty rather than enforce the reviewer's preferred stance. |
| REV-V4 Format constraints | Verified word, reference, figure or supplement limits restrict the format | Prioritize changes needed for accurate interpretation; consider replacing less important material, narrowing scope or using permitted supplements. Do not require impossible additions or trade away evidence integrity merely to fit. Name a genuine format/claim conflict for editorial handling. |

## Soft defaults — synthesis and communication

| ID | Starting question | Adaptation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| REV-S1 Scope and balance | Is coverage sufficiently representative for the stated question, including relevant conflicting evidence, foundational work and important updates? | A focused review need not cover the entire field. Judge deliberate boundaries and limitations rather than demand maximum breadth or an arbitrary recency cutoff. |
| REV-S2 Accurate concepts | Are concepts, terms, assumptions and boundaries defined consistently, and could the intended reader understand the topic correctly? | Preserve necessary technical precision. Accessibility depends on audience, not a universal requirement to write for beginners. Use READ rules for consequential ambiguity. |
| REV-S3 Synthesis over listing | Does the article connect evidence, explain agreements/disagreements and distinguish what is known from open questions? | An educational review may contribute through clear organization; a critical review may contribute through argument. Neither requires a provocative claim or forced consensus. |
| REV-S4 Distinct contribution | How does it relate to relevant recent reviews: changed evidence, different interpretation, scope, framework or audience? | Check actual comparators before claiming redundancy. A different audience can justify a useful review when venue criteria permit; cosmetic rewriting does not establish novelty. Use INT rules for actual overlap concerns. |
| REV-S5 Useful structure | Do headings, explanatory figures/tables and appropriate length support the intended purpose? | Suggest bounded improvements; do not become a copyeditor or formatting police. Apply FIG rules to scientific displays and LANG rules to the report's wording. |

## Output and priorities

Record the synthesis type, declared scope, intended audience, literature/search
cutoff where reported, applicable guidance actually checked and limits of external
source verification. Map pivotal synthesis claims to cited evidence and inspected
locations. Record strengths as well as supported concerns in the usual workbook.

As an S-level recommendation aid, imagine a reader from the actual intended
audience: can they understand the account, obtain an accurate and appropriately
current view within its declared cutoff, and identify its contribution relative
to relevant alternatives? The slide's interested non-expert is one useful example,
not the required reader for every specialist review. Do not equate usefulness
with a mandatory unique discovery or use this heuristic as an acceptance score.

Prioritize by consequence: misrepresenting a central source or presenting a
contested hypothesis as settled may be major; a localized definition may be minor.
Neither label follows automatically from topic or section. Offer the smallest
adequate remedy: correction, clearer scope/selection rationale, bounded source
coverage, revised synthesis or narrowed conclusion. New experiments usually fall
outside a review article's purpose; do not require them without a specific reason
tied to retained claims and applicable criteria.

For journals offering accept/minor/major/reject, connect advice to actual category
definitions and explain what is already adequate, what must change and whether
the remedy is feasible. A figure correction or added discussion is not necessarily
minor; consequences determine priority. Do not require language/format edits as
proof that a paper cannot be accepted, or assume that rejection is justified only
by irreparable flaws: an applicable venue criterion may also be unmet. Conference
tracks retain their own rubric and permitted revision stages.

Use the actual journal or conference report form and stage rules. Reviewing a
review article does not change who decides, what may be disclosed or who submits.
