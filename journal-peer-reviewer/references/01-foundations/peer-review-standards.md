# Peer-review terminology and policy

## Authority and scope

**ANSI/NISO Z39.106-2023, Standard Terminology for Peer Review**, approved 2023-06-05 and published 2023-07-05, DOI [10.3789/ansi.niso.z39.106-2023](https://doi.org/10.3789/ansi.niso.z39.106-2023). The official [publication page](https://www.niso.org/publications/z39106-2023-peerreview), [standard PDF](https://groups.niso.org/higherlogic/ws/public/download/28900/Z39.106-2023-Peer_Review_Terminology.pdf), and [committee page](https://www.niso.org/standards-committees/peer-review-terminology) were inspected on 2026-10-22.

The standard's Foreword describes the STM initiative and its continuation as a NISO working group. NISO also records the joint NISO/STM launch. The STM taxonomy endpoint returned HTTP 403 during this check; the standard itself supplies the primary terminology. Do not present this as two independently verified standards.

Section 1 explicitly scopes this edition to **external peer review of journal articles**. Conference proceedings are a possible later extension, not covered by that edition's scope. Scientific criteria (novelty, impact, methods) and the acceptance decision-making process are outside its scope. Consequently:

- It is a terminology standard, not a quality score, a validation certificate, or an AI-use permission.
- The conference skill may reuse the dimensions as a descriptive aid but must not claim that the standard prescribes conference reviewing.
- The 2023 publication was verified; no comprehensive standards-register audit establishing it as the latest edition was performed. Recheck current status and actual venue policy for each assignment.

## Four dimensions — keep them separate

| Standard section | Terms and operational interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| §2.1 **identity transparency** | **all identities visible**, **single anonymized**, **double anonymized**, **triple anonymized**. Single: reviewer hidden from author; double: author and reviewer hidden from each other; triple: both also hidden from decision-making editor. Record exceptional roles/stages separately. |
| §2.2 **reviewer interacts with** | **editor**, **other reviewers**, **authors**; multiple can apply. Record permitted direct exchanges and timing. Anonymous review can include interaction. |
| §2.3 **review information published** | Record applicable items: **none**, **review summaries**, **review reports**, reports with author/reviewer opt-in, **submitted manuscript** (including author opt-in), **author/editor communication**, **reviewer identities** (including reviewer opt-in), **editor identities**. Publication of reports does not by itself reveal reviewer identities. |
| §2.4 **post publication commenting** | **open** or **on invitation**, when applicable, for commenting on the published version of record on the publishing platform. This excludes third-party services such as PubPeer and standalone comment/reply/letter articles. |

This table summarizes the standard; consult its definitions for precise implementation. “Unknown” and “not applicable” are local record statuses, not additional NISO terms. In particular, do not record “none” as the standard's post-publication category. “Open peer review” alone does not specify all four dimensions. Prefer “anonymized” terminology rather than “blind.”

Distinguish visibility during review (§2.1) from identity publication afterward (§2.3). A double-anonymized process can publish reports later. Availability on OpenReview does not establish who can see a particular form/comment at a particular stage.

## Normative versus informative material

Appendix A is explicitly informative. It offers communication examples and encourages disclosure of transfers/cascades, dates, rounds, fast-tracking, report counts, and technical tools including AI/ML. These are not a normative requirement that every reviewer provide every field, nor permission to use such tools.

Our workbook records these dimensions to avoid wrong assumptions about audiences, interaction, and disclosures. It does not certify a journal, conference, manuscript, or reviewer as NISO compliant. COPE/venue ethics guidance, reporting standards, confidentiality rules, and scientific appraisal remain separate responsibilities.
