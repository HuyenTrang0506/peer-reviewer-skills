# Professional language rules

Reusable for review comments, editorial notes, office email, memos and reports.
These local H/V/S classes concern expression; they do not supply scientific or
organizational authority. Use the user's language and audience where established.

## Hard rules

| ID | Trigger | Requirement and recovery |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LANG-H1 Preserve meaning | Editing or translating supplied text | Preserve factual claims, numerical values/units, uncertainty, negation, attribution, obligations and intent. Do not soften “required” into “optional” or turn association into causation. Flag ambiguity instead of inventing the intended meaning. |
| LANG-H2 No invented commitments | Drafting a message | Do not invent facts, approvals, credentials, deadlines, promises, completed checks, citations or recipient actions. Use an explicit placeholder or a bounded question when essential information is missing. |
| LANG-H3 Respectful precision | Criticism, disagreement or requests | Address observable work/behavior and consequences without insult, discriminatory language, threats or guessed motives. Retain justified negative conclusions; courtesy must not conceal a required correction. |
| LANG-H4 Audience and handling | Confidential or restricted content | Preserve the supplied audience and handling limits. Drafting does not authorize sending, publishing, changing files, or copying restricted content to another channel/service. If audience is unresolved and matters, prepare separated drafts with the unresolved destination explicit. |

## Conditional requirements

| ID | Trigger | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LANG-V1 Required terminology/format | Applicable user, venue or organization instructions specify language, terms, formality, word limits or mandatory disclosure | Follow the actual instruction and source. Do not claim compliance with an unseen house style. If wording would misrepresent facts, flag the conflict and propose an accurate alternative. |

## Soft defaults

| ID | Default | Adaptation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LANG-S1 Direct structure | Lead with the purpose or finding, then evidence/reason, requested action and next step if known. | Match the form, culture and document length; omit unnecessary headings or repeated summaries. |
| LANG-S2 Plain language | Prefer specific verbs, short connected sentences and defined technical terms. | Retain specialist vocabulary when it is more precise for the audience; avoid expanding text merely to sound formal. |
| LANG-S3 Calibrated firmness | Distinguish observation, interpretation, uncertainty and request. Keep required and optional actions visibly different. | Use a firm statement for an established issue and conditional wording for an unverified one. Do not add blanket hedges to every sentence. |
| LANG-S4 Minimal editing | Change only what improves meaning, tone or usability; preserve the writer's voice. | A requested rewrite may change structure more substantially. Explain only consequential changes or unresolved ambiguity, not every grammar correction. |

## Original examples

| Unhelpful wording | Evidence-bounded alternative |
| --- | --- |
| “The authors do not understand statistics.” | “Methods §2 does not identify the independent unit. Please clarify it and how repeated observations were handled.” |
| “Your figure is terrible.” | “The colorbar labels are unreadable in the supplied rendering, so the mapped values cannot be interpreted. Please provide a legible scale and define its units.” |
| “Your team ignored the deadline.” | “The report was due on [confirmed date] and has not been received. Please confirm its status and expected delivery date.” |

Use examples only when supported by the actual task. Bracketed fields must be
resolved or clearly retained as placeholders; do not invent their contents.
